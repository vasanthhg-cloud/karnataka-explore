About Gulbarga Fort

A large fort founded in the 14th century by Ala-ud-din Bahman Shah and rebuilt by the Bahmani sultans, with 15 towers, a deep moat and the Jama Masjid inside, whose great dome was modelled on the mosque at Cordoba. The fort's later Mughal and Asaf Jahi additions show four centuries of Deccan politics in one enclosure.

Kalaburagi

Khwaja Bande Nawaz Dargah

The tomb of the 14th-century Sufi saint Hazrat Khwaja Bande Nawaz Gesu Daraz, one of the most visited shrines in south India. The dargah complex includes a mosque, a library and a large courtyard, and the annual urs draws pilgrims from across India. Its architecture blends Bahmani, Mughal and Persian elements.

Sharana Basaveshwara Temple

A Lingayat temple dedicated to Sharana Basaveshwara, a 19th-century saint and social reformer, set around a large tank in the middle of Kalaburagi. The temple runs a math that supports education, and its annual jatra fills the tank side with devotees.

Buddha Vihar, Kalaburagi

A large Buddhist monastery and meditation centre built in the 1980s in the Ambedkar tradition, with a towering statue of the Buddha and a hall used for meditation and study. It is one of the biggest Buddhist viharas in Karnataka and a calm contrast to the fort and dargah circuit.

Chitradurga

Chitradurga Fort

A massive seven-walled fort built in stages by the Nayakas of Chitradurga between the 15th and 18th centuries, wrapping around a cluster of granite hills. Its 19 gateways, 38 bastions and ingenious water tanks are cut straight into the rock, and the Onake Obavva kindi, a narrow crevice in the wall, commemorates the woman who defended it single-handed. The fort was taken by Hyder Ali in 1779.
