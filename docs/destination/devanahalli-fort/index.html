Devanahalli Fort

Devanahalli town, 35 km from Bengaluru Bengaluru Rural 1-2 hours

About Devanahalli Fort

A 15th-century fort town built by Malla Baire Gowda and later rebuilt in stone by Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. Within the oval ramparts stand the Venugopala Swamy temple and a cluster of old houses, and the fort is also the birthplace of Tipu Sultan, whose family home is marked nearby. It is an easy half-day escape from Bengaluru on the airport road.

Devanahalli is on NH-44 near Kempegowda International Airport. Buses and suburban trains from Bengaluru reach Devanahalli in about an hour.

Bengaluru Rural

Nrityagram Dance Village

A residential dance village founded in 1990 by Odissi dancer Protima Gauri, where students live and train in classical Indian dance in mud-and-thatch buildings set among fields. Visitors can tour the campus, watch morning practice, and attend the annual Vasanta Habba festival held in the adjoining gardens.

Bengaluru Rural

Hessarghatta Lake

An artificial lake built in 1894 to supply drinking water to Bengaluru, now a dry-season grassland that has become one of the best birding spots near the city. The surrounding scrub and lake bed attract harriers, larks, sandgrouse and the near-threatened Lesser Florican, and the open horizon makes it a favourite with photographers.

Chitradurga

Chitradurga Fort

A massive seven-walled fort built in stages by the Nayakas of Chitradurga between the 15th and 18th centuries, wrapping around a cluster of granite hills. Its 19 gateways, 38 bastions and ingenious water tanks are cut straight into the rock, and the Onake Obavva kindi, a narrow crevice in the wall, commemorates the woman who defended it single-handed. The fort was taken by Hyder Ali in 1779.

Ballari

Ballari Fort

A two-part granite fort built on a rocky hill above Ballari city. The lower fort is attributed to Hanumappa Nayaka of the Vijayanagara period, while the upper fort was strengthened by Hyder Ali in the 18th century, who is said to have used French engineers to improve the ramparts. The climb gives a wide view of the city and the surrounding iron-ore country.
