Hessarghatta Lake

Hesaraghatta, Bengaluru North Bengaluru Rural 2-3 hours

About Hessarghatta Lake

An artificial lake built in 1894 to supply drinking water to Bengaluru, now a dry-season grassland that has become one of the best birding spots near the city. The surrounding scrub and lake bed attract harriers, larks, sandgrouse and the near-threatened Lesser Florican, and the open horizon makes it a favourite with photographers.

Bengaluru Rural

Devanahalli Fort

A 15th-century fort town built by Malla Baire Gowda and later rebuilt in stone by Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. Within the oval ramparts stand the Venugopala Swamy temple and a cluster of old houses, and the fort is also the birthplace of Tipu Sultan, whose family home is marked nearby. It is an easy half-day escape from Bengaluru on the airport road.

Bengaluru Rural

Nrityagram Dance Village

A residential dance village founded in 1990 by Odissi dancer Protima Gauri, where students live and train in classical Indian dance in mud-and-thatch buildings set among fields. Visitors can tour the campus, watch morning practice, and attend the annual Vasanta Habba festival held in the adjoining gardens.

Mandya

Brindavan Gardens, KRS

Terraced gardens laid out below the Krishnaraja Sagar dam by Sir Mirza Ismail in the 1930s, with fountains, canals and an amphitheatre. The musical fountain show at dusk, when water jets dance to music under coloured light, is the main draw, and the gardens are a standard day trip from Mysuru.

Bengaluru Urban

Lalbagh Botanical Garden

A 240-acre botanical garden laid out by Hyder Ali in 1760 and completed by Tipu Sultan, with one of India's largest collections of tropical plants. The glasshouse modelled on Crystal Palace hosts the famous biannual flower show, and the garden's centrepiece is a 3,000-million-year-old rock formation. Early mornings bring walkers, birdwatchers and yoga groups.
