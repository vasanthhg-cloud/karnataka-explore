About Gavimath

A cave temple and math in the hills outside Koppal, associated with the 12th-century Sharana tradition and with Siddharama, the Lingayat saint and social reformer. The site has rock-cut shrines, a spring and inscriptions, and it draws pilgrims during the annual jatra.

Forts Free entry
Koppal

Koppal Fort

A hill fort on a granite outcrop, held in turn by the Chalukyas, the Vijayanagara kings, Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. The climb of about 400 steps passes through gateways and bastions to the summit, where the view stretches over the dry plains of Koppal and the Tungabhadra valley.

Koppal

Mahadeva Temple, Itagi

A 12th-century Kalyani Chalukya temple that an inscription calls the emperor among temples, with a hall of lathe-turned pillars, a richly carved doorway and a dome with finely cut ceiling panels. It is regarded as the finest example of the later Chalukyan style and is a protected monument.

Koppal

Kanakagiri Jain Temple

A group of Jain temples on a granite hill, known for the shrine of Kanakagiri Tirthankara and for the rock-cut images and inscriptions in the surrounding hills. The site was an important Jain centre under the Rashtrakutas and Chalukyas and is still visited by pilgrims from across Karnataka.

Bagalkote

Pattadakal Group of Monuments

A UNESCO World Heritage Site where the Chalukya kings built a row of temples in the 7th and 8th centuries to mark their coronations. Nine Hindu temples and a Jain sanctuary stand together on the bank of the Malaprabha, showing North Indian and South Indian styles side by side in the same compound. The Virupaksha Temple is the largest and still active; the Papanatha and Sangameshwara temples show the experiment of blending both traditions.
