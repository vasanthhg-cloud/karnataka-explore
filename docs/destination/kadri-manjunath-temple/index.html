Kadri Manjunath Temple

Kadri, Mangaluru Dakshina Kannada 45 minutes

About Kadri Manjunath Temple

A temple with origins in the 10th century, rebuilt in the 14th, dedicated to Manjunatha and important to the Nath tradition of Shaivism. Its bronze image of Lokeshwara, cast in the 10th century, is regarded as one of the finest bronzes in south India, and the temple tank and the hill behind it are part of the visit.

Beaches Free entry
Dakshina Kannada

Panambur Beach

A broad, clean beach beside Mangaluru's port, known for its well-organised waterfront with lifeguards, beach shacks and the annual kite festival in December. Sunsets over the Arabian Sea are the main attraction, and the beach is a short drive from the city centre.

Dakshina Kannada

Sultan Battery

A small watchtower and battery built by Tipu Sultan in 1784 to block the entry of warships into the Gurupura river. Built of stone blocks with a spiral staircase, it stands on the riverbank and now offers a quiet spot for sunset views across the water towards the port.

Uttara Kannada

Murudeshwar Temple

A temple town on the coast where a 37-metre statue of Shiva stands on a hill above the sea, with a 20-storey gopura behind it. The temple is believed to hold a piece of the Atma Linga from the Ramayana story, and the lift inside the gopura takes visitors up for a view along the coastline.

Bagalkote

Aihole Durga Temple Complex

Aihole is called the cradle of Indian temple architecture: over 120 temples in and around the village record how the Chalukyan builders invented and refined the South Indian temple form between the 6th and 12th centuries. The Durga Temple, with its apsidal plan and richly carved pillars, is the best known, and the nearby Lad Khan, Konti Gudi and Meguti hill shrines reward a slow walk.
