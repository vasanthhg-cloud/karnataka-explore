Madhugiri town, Tumakuru district Tumakuru ★★★☆☆ 3.0 (1) 3-4 hours

About Madhugiri Fort

A fort on the second-largest monolith in Asia, with a steep climb of stone steps cut into the rock face and gateways at intervals. Built by the local chief Raja Hera Gauda and later taken by Hyder Ali, the fort's walls and granaries cling to the rock, and the summit view is over the whole Tumakuru plain.

Devarayanadurga

A forested hill range with two temples, the Yoga Narasimha and Bhoga Narasimha shrines, at about 1,200 metres, surrounded by a reserve forest. The drive up through the forest is pleasant, monkeys are common, and the hill is a popular day trip from Bengaluru and Tumakuru.

Tumakuru

Namada Chilume

A small natural spring that flows from a rock at the foot of the Devarayanadurga hills, traditionally said to be the place where Rama marked the rock with his toe to draw water. The site has a deer park and a garden, and the spring runs even in the dry season.

Cultural Heritage Free entry
Tumakuru

Siddaganga Math

A major Lingayat monastery and centre of education, famous for running free hostels and schools for thousands of students regardless of caste or religion. The math was led for decades by Shivakumara Swamiji, and its campus with the main shrine and the annual jatra is a major gathering point.

Chitradurga

Chitradurga Fort

A massive seven-walled fort built in stages by the Nayakas of Chitradurga between the 15th and 18th centuries, wrapping around a cluster of granite hills. Its 19 gateways, 38 bastions and ingenious water tanks are cut straight into the rock, and the Onake Obavva kindi, a narrow crevice in the wall, commemorates the woman who defended it single-handed. The fort was taken by Hyder Ali in 1779.
