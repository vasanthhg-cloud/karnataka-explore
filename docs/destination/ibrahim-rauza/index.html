About Ibrahim Rauza

A twin tomb and mosque complex built in the 1620s by Ibrahim Adil Shah II for his queen Taj Sultana and himself, often described as the high point of Adil Shahi architecture. The delicate minarets, the carved brackets and the surrounding garden make it more elegant than Gol Gumbaz, though far less visited.

Vijayapura

Gol Gumbaz

The mausoleum of Muhammad Adil Shah, completed in 1656, with the second-largest pre-modern dome in the world after St. Peter's. The Whispering Gallery at the base of the dome carries a whisper across 37 metres, and the building's dark basalt and simple geometry make it one of the finest pieces of Deccan architecture.

Vijayapura

Malik-e-Maidan

A colossal cannon cast in 1549 at Ahmadnagar and brought to Bijapur as a trophy, weighing about 55 tonnes and over 4 metres long. It stands on the Sherza Burj of the old city wall, and its muzzle is said to be shaped like a lion's head with an elephant being crushed. It is one of the largest medieval cannons in the world.

Vijayapura

Jumma Masjid, Vijayapura

A large mosque begun by Ali Adil Shah I in the 16th century, with a grand prayer hall, a vast dome and a courtyard with a reservoir. Its mihrab and calligraphy are notable, and the incomplete state of parts of the building records the interrupted fortunes of the Adil Shahi dynasty.

Historical Places ₹25
Bagalkote

Badami Cave Temples

Four rock-cut cave temples carved into the sandstone cliffs above Agastya Lake between the 6th and 8th centuries, when Badami was the capital of the Early Chalukyas. Cave 1 is Shaiva, Caves 2 and 3 are Vaishnava and Cave 4 is Jain. Cave 3, dated 578 CE, holds some of the finest early Indian sculpture, including the eighteen-armed Nataraja and Vishnu seated on Ananta. The red sandstone cliffs and the lake below make the whole complex remarkably photogenic.
