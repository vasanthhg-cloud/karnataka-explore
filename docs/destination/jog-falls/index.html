Jog Falls

Sagara taluk, Shivamogga district Shivamogga ★★★★☆ 4.0 (2) 2 hours

About Jog Falls

India's second-highest plunge waterfall, where the Sharavati drops about 253 metres in four distinct cascades named Raja, Rani, Roarer and Rocket. It is spectacular in the monsoon when the falls run full, and in the dry season the river is diverted to the Linganamakki reservoir and the flow is much reduced. A long flight of steps leads from the viewpoint down to the base.

More around Shivamogga

Nature and Eco-Tourism Free entry
Shivamogga

Agumbe

A hill village in the Western Ghats that records some of the highest rainfall in south India, and the site of the Agumbe Rainforest Research Station. It is known for king cobras, for the sunset viewpoint at a cliff over the coastal plain, and for the ancient Gopalakrishna temple. The surrounding forest is a biodiversity hotspot.

Sakrebailu Elephant Camp

A forest camp on the Tunga river where trained elephants are kept by the Forest Department, and visitors can watch them being fed and bathed in the river. It is a low-key, inexpensive stop close to Shivamogga city, popular with families.

Cultural Heritage Free entry
Shivamogga

Kuppalli (Kuvempu's home)

The birthplace of the Kannada poet and Jnanpith laureate Kuvempu, now a memorial with his house, a museum of his belongings and a research centre set among areca and paddy fields. The poet's epic Sri Ramayana Darshanam is closely associated with this landscape, and the site is a pilgrimage for Kannada readers.

Kodagu

Abbey Falls

A waterfall on the Kaveri's tributary about 9 km from Madikeri, where the stream drops through coffee and spice plantations into a rocky pool. A hanging bridge opposite the falls gives a full view, and the walk down passes through cardamom and pepper vines. It is at its heaviest in the monsoon.
