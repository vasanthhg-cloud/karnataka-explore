Maski, Lingasugur taluk Raichur 1-2 hours

About Maski

A prehistoric and early historic site on the Maski river where a rock edict of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka was discovered in 1915, the first to name him as Devanampiya Piyadasi. Excavations have revealed Neolithic ash mounds, megalithic burials and a long sequence of occupation, making it one of the most important archaeological sites in south India.

Raichur

Raichur Fort

A fort on a rocky hill above the Tungabhadra, contested for centuries between the Vijayanagara empire and the Bahmani sultanate because of its position between the Krishna and Tungabhadra doabs. The walls, gateways and a Persian inscription on the gate record its many rebuildings, and the summit gives a wide view of the river and the plains.

Mudgal Fort

A fort of the Bahmani and Adil Shahi periods with a long stretch of surviving walls, bastions and gateways, set in dry farmland about 40 km from Raichur. Mudgal was an important Deccan stronghold and its fortifications, though overgrown, are among the better preserved in the region.

Bagalkote

Badami Cave Temples

Four rock-cut cave temples carved into the sandstone cliffs above Agastya Lake between the 6th and 8th centuries, when Badami was the capital of the Early Chalukyas. Cave 1 is Shaiva, Caves 2 and 3 are Vaishnava and Cave 4 is Jain. Cave 3, dated 578 CE, holds some of the finest early Indian sculpture, including the eighteen-armed Nataraja and Vishnu seated on Ananta. The red sandstone cliffs and the lake below make the whole complex remarkably photogenic.

Mysuru

Mysore Palace

The seat of the Wodeyar dynasty and one of India's most visited monuments after the Taj Mahal. The present palace was completed in 1912 by Henry Irwin after a fire, in a blend of Hindu, Islamic, Gothic and Rajput styles, with a 145-foot dome. Inside are the Durbar Hall, the golden throne and a large collection of paintings; on Sunday evenings and during Dasara the whole facade is lit with nearly 100,000 bulbs.
