Kodagu

Abbey Falls

A waterfall on the Kaveri's tributary about 9 km from Madikeri, where the stream drops through coffee and spice plantations into a rocky pool. A hanging bridge opposite the falls gives a full view, and the walk down passes through cardamom and pepper vines. It is at its heaviest in the monsoon.

Hill Stations ₹15
Chikkaballapura

Nandi Hills

A 1,478-metre granite hill that was Tipu Sultan's summer retreat and is now Bengaluru's favourite sunrise drive. Tipu's Drop, a 600-metre cliff, the old summer palace, the Bhoga Nandishwara temple at the foot and the mist that settles on the ridge at dawn are the main draws. The hill fort has been restored and the road to the top is now metalled.

Historical Places ₹25
Bagalkote

Badami Cave Temples

Four rock-cut cave temples carved into the sandstone cliffs above Agastya Lake between the 6th and 8th centuries, when Badami was the capital of the Early Chalukyas. Cave 1 is Shaiva, Caves 2 and 3 are Vaishnava and Cave 4 is Jain. Cave 3, dated 578 CE, holds some of the finest early Indian sculpture, including the eighteen-armed Nataraja and Vishnu seated on Ananta. The red sandstone cliffs and the lake below make the whole complex remarkably photogenic.

Waterfalls ₹20
Shivamogga

Jog Falls

India's second-highest plunge waterfall, where the Sharavati drops about 253 metres in four distinct cascades named Raja, Rani, Roarer and Rocket. It is spectacular in the monsoon when the falls run full, and in the dry season the river is diverted to the Linganamakki reservoir and the flow is much reduced. A long flight of steps leads from the viewpoint down to the base.

Uttara Kannada

Murudeshwar Temple

A temple town on the coast where a 37-metre statue of Shiva stands on a hill above the sea, with a 20-storey gopura behind it. The temple is believed to hold a piece of the Atma Linga from the Ramayana story, and the lift inside the gopura takes visitors up for a view along the coastline.

Historical Places ₹100
Mysuru

Mysore Palace

The seat of the Wodeyar dynasty and one of India's most visited monuments after the Taj Mahal. The present palace was completed in 1912 by Henry Irwin after a fire, in a blend of Hindu, Islamic, Gothic and Rajput styles, with a 145-foot dome. Inside are the Durbar Hall, the golden throne and a large collection of paintings; on Sunday evenings and during Dasara the whole facade is lit with nearly 100,000 bulbs.

Waterfalls ₹20
Belagavi

Gokak Falls

The Ghataprabha river drops about 52 metres over a horseshoe-shaped sandstone cliff just outside Gokak town, giving one of the widest waterfalls in Karnataka. It is at its best in the monsoon and immediately after, when the whole rock face is white with water. A hanging suspension bridge over the gorge, a Chalukyan temple on the cliff, and the 1887 Gokak Mills hydro-electric station add interest.

Waterfalls ₹20
Chamarajanagara

Bharachukki Falls

The eastern branch of the Kaveri at Shivanasamudra, where the river breaks into a wide curtain of falls about 90 metres high. Coracle rides run to the base of the falls in season, and the viewpoint above gives a full view of the gorge. The western branch nearby is Gaganachukki; together they are among the largest falls in the state.

Temples ₹25
Bagalkote

Aihole Durga Temple Complex

Aihole is called the cradle of Indian temple architecture: over 120 temples in and around the village record how the Chalukyan builders invented and refined the South Indian temple form between the 6th and 12th centuries. The Durga Temple, with its apsidal plan and richly carved pillars, is the best known, and the nearby Lad Khan, Konti Gudi and Meguti hill shrines reward a slow walk.

Beaches and hill stations

Beaches Free entry
Uttara Kannada

Om Beach, Gokarna

A beach shaped like the Om symbol, with two crescents of sand and rocky headlands, reached by a short walk from Gokarna town. It is the most accessible of Gokarna's five beaches, with shacks, water sports and a steady flow of travellers, and the cliff path to Half Moon and Paradise beaches is a fine walk.

Beaches ₹10
Udupi

Kaup Beach and Lighthouse

A rocky beach with a 27-metre lighthouse built in 1901 on a black rock outcrop, which can be climbed for a view of the coast. The beach is quieter than Malpe and the rocks and tide pools make it good for photography, especially at sunset.

Chamarajanagara

Himavad Gopalaswamy Betta

The highest hill in the Bandipur range at about 1,450 metres, topped by a temple to Krishna as Gopalaswamy that sits inside a small fort. The drive up passes through forest, and on clear days the view over the Wayanad and Nilgiri ranges is superb. The hill is famous for the mist that rolls over it for much of the year, which gives it the name Himavad, or snow-capped.
