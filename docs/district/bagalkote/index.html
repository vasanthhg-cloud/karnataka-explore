Bagalkote

Badami Cave Temples

Four rock-cut cave temples carved into the sandstone cliffs above Agastya Lake between the 6th and 8th centuries, when Badami was the capital of the Early Chalukyas. Cave 1 is Shaiva, Caves 2 and 3 are Vaishnava and Cave 4 is Jain. Cave 3, dated 578 CE, holds some of the finest early Indian sculpture, including the eighteen-armed Nataraja and Vishnu seated on Ananta. The red sandstone cliffs and the lake below make the whole complex remarkably photogenic.

Pattadakal Group of Monuments

A UNESCO World Heritage Site where the Chalukya kings built a row of temples in the 7th and 8th centuries to mark their coronations. Nine Hindu temples and a Jain sanctuary stand together on the bank of the Malaprabha, showing North Indian and South Indian styles side by side in the same compound. The Virupaksha Temple is the largest and still active; the Papanatha and Sangameshwara temples show the experiment of blending both traditions.

Aihole Durga Temple Complex

Aihole is called the cradle of Indian temple architecture: over 120 temples in and around the village record how the Chalukyan builders invented and refined the South Indian temple form between the 6th and 12th centuries. The Durga Temple, with its apsidal plan and richly carved pillars, is the best known, and the nearby Lad Khan, Konti Gudi and Meguti hill shrines reward a slow walk.

Kudala Sangama

The confluence of the Krishna and Malaprabha rivers, and one of the most important Lingayat pilgrimage centres in Karnataka. The Aikya Mantapa here marks the site where the 12th-century poet-saint Basavanna's mortal remains are believed to have merged with the linga. The Sangamanatha temple stands on the riverbank and the Almatti dam reservoir spreads out behind it.
