Hassan

Chennakeshava Temple, Belur

The masterpiece of Hoysala architecture, begun in 1117 CE by King Vishnuvardhana to mark his conversion from Jainism to Vaishnavism. Every inch of the exterior is covered with sculpture: bracket figures of dancers, friezes of elephants, and narrative panels from the epics. The temple is still in worship and is on India's UNESCO tentative list.

Hoysaleswara Temple, Halebidu

The largest Hoysala temple, begun around 1121 CE and never finished, with twin shrines to Shiva and a sculpted exterior in bands of elephants, lions, scrollwork and mythological scenes. The famous wall panels of Ravana lifting Kailasa and of Arjuna shooting the fish are here, along with two monolithic Nandi bulls. Halebidu was the Hoysala capital until it was sacked in the 14th century.

Hassan

Shettihalli Rosary Church

The roofless shell of a 19th-century French-built church that is submerged for part of the year by the Hemavathi reservoir and emerges when the water drops. The exposed brick arches standing in the water or in the cracked mud flats make it one of the most photographed ruins in Karnataka, especially at sunset.

Cultural Heritage Free entry
Hassan

Shravanabelagola

One of the most important Jain pilgrimage sites in India, dominated by the 17-metre monolithic statue of Bahubali on Vindhyagiri hill, carved in 981 CE. Pilgrims climb 620 steps cut into the granite to reach it, and the smaller Chandragiri hill opposite holds a cluster of ancient shrines and inscriptions. The Mahamastakabhisheka head-anointing ceremony is held roughly every twelve years.
