Anegundi

The older settlement across the Tungabhadra from Hampi, said to be the monkey kingdom of Kishkindha in the Ramayana and the first capital of the Vijayanagara empire. It has a fort, the Ranganatha temple, ancient rock art in the surrounding hills, and a rural craft centre run by a local NGO. A coracle ride across the river links it to Hampi.

Vijayanagara

Lotus Mahal and Zenana Enclosure

A two-storeyed pavilion in the royal enclosure with arched openings and a plaster finish that suggests Indo-Islamic influence, thought to have been a pleasure pavilion for the royal women. It stands among watchtowers and the ruins of the zenana quarters, and the elephant stables nearby are among the best-preserved buildings at Hampi.

Vijayanagara

Virupaksha Temple, Hampi

The oldest and most important temple at Hampi, in continuous worship since the 7th century and enlarged by the Vijayanagara emperors into a complex with a 50-metre gopura, colonnaded bazaars and a riverside ghat. It is the only major temple at Hampi never abandoned, and the evening puja with the temple elephant is a daily ritual.

Vijayanagara

Tungabhadra Dam and Garden, Hosapete

A large dam across the Tungabhadra built in the 1950s, with terraced gardens, fountains and a musical fountain on the downstream side. The reservoir stretches towards Hampi, and the garden is a popular evening spot with a view of the dam wall and the river below.

Cultural Heritage ₹40
Vijayanagara

Vittala Temple and Stone Chariot

The most ornate monument at Hampi, with a hall of 56 musical pillars that ring when struck, and the stone chariot that has become the symbol of Karnataka tourism. Built in the 16th century, the temple's carved pillars, gopura and the adjacent bazaar street show Vijayanagara sculpture at its peak. The chariot's wheels were once free-turning.
